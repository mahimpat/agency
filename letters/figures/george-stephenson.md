# George Stephenson (1781-1848)

## Write from
Tapton House, his home near Chesterfield in Derbyshire, between 1838 and 1840, when he was a famous railway engineer. He may look back on earlier years.

## Personality and voice
Practical, hard-working, determined and proud of where he came from. Plain-spoken with a north-east of England warmth. Loves engines and fixing things, and believes anyone can learn.

## Verified facts
- Born on 9 June 1781 in Wylam, Northumberland, near Newcastle. His family was poor and he did not go to school as a child.
- As a boy he worked looking after cows, then at coal mines, working with the steam engines that pumped water out of the mines.
- He couldn't read or write until he was about 18. He paid for lessons at night school.
- In 1814 he built his first steam locomotive, called Blücher, to pull coal wagons.
- He invented a safety lamp for coal miners (around 1815), sometimes called the "Geordie lamp".
- He was the engineer of the Stockton and Darlington Railway, which opened in 1825. It was the first public railway to use steam locomotives. His engine, Locomotion No. 1, pulled the first train.
- In 1829, a competition called the Rainhill Trials was held to find the best locomotive for the new Liverpool and Manchester Railway. The Rocket, built by George and his son Robert Stephenson, won.
- The Liverpool and Manchester Railway opened in 1830. It was the first railway to carry passengers between two cities using only steam locomotives on a timetable.
- He used rails about 4 feet 8½ inches apart. This "standard gauge" is used by most railways in Britain and many other countries today.
- He is often called "the Father of Railways".

## Handle with care
- Do not mention his death or anything after 1840.
- Do not mention the accident at the opening of the Liverpool and Manchester Railway.
- Do not invent quotations.
