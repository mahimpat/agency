The idea

A pen pal from history who actually listens

Children aged 5 to 12 get beautifully printed letters through the letterbox from figures like Mary Anning, Isambard Kingdom Brunel and Ada Lovelace. Each letter is written for that child. When the child writes back, the pen pal answers their actual questions.

Why families will pay

  • Screen-free and real. Paper, ink and a letter with their name on it.
  • It gets children writing. Few things motivate a reluctant writer like a question from a pen pal.
  • History that sticks. Every letter comes with a "Real history" card for parent and child to read together.
  • Perfect gift. Grandparents are a natural second audience.

Why we're different

  • Existing services (Heritage Letter, Letters From Afar, History By Mail; Banjo Robinson in the UK) send the same letter to every child. We found none that answers a child's own letter.
  • AI makes truly personal, two-way letters affordable. People check them.
  • Every pen pal writes on stationery from their own era, so each letter feels like a real object.

Why it can run with a small team

AI agents write, fact-check and lay out every letter. Stannp prints and posts them through Royal Mail, and Stripe takes payments. The team's daily job is reviewing: approving letters and handling any child's letter that needs a person. That takes minutes a day, not hours.

A friendship, not a series of leaflets

  • Introduction: the first letter from every new pen pal.
  • Story: a new chapter of their life, linked to the child's interests.
  • Reply: answers every question in the child's letter.
  • Follow-up: if no reply comes within 3 weeks.
  • Farewell: after 3 letters, a goodbye. "Next month, a new pen pal from another time will write to you."

Accuracy rules every letter follows

  • Only facts from the figure's fact sheet. No invented dates, events or quotations.
  • Pen pals never know their own future, so there are no spoilers about their deaths.
  • Anything imaginative (e.g. letters travelling through time) is marked on the Real history card.
  • British English, at the child's reading level.

Why this design

Each AI step has one job, and the fact-checker is separate from the writer, so mistakes get caught rather than repeated. Every letter's fact-check record is saved, which lets us back up the claim that "every fact is checked".

What the AI sees

The child's first name, age, interests and the words of their reply. It never sees surnames, addresses, emails or the parent's details.

Letter types and rotation

Which pen pal a child has, and whether the next letter is an introduction, story or farewell, is worked out from the letters already sent. Rejected letters never throw the rotation off.

Daily job

Once a day a scheduler triggers the run: read new replies, write the letters that payments allow, and queue them for review. Letters a parent hasn't responded to within 48 hours are posted.

Safeguarding

Keeping children safe

Children will sometimes tell a pen pal things they haven't told anyone. We designed for that from the start, and we treat it as the most important part of the product.

LayerWhat it does
Parents onlyChildren have no accounts and no contact with anyone. Only a parent or guardian can sign up and upload replies.
AI safeguarding assessmentEvery reply is graded none, low, high or urgent. It also flags inappropriate content, attempts to instruct the AI, and letters that don't look like a child wrote them.
Word screen (no AI)Words about being hurt, self-harm, secrets, fear of an adult, running away, neglect or bullying force a hold even if the AI says the letter is fine. Tested against exactly that case.
Personal details removedAddresses, phone numbers, emails, postcodes and social media handles are stripped out before anything is stored or written.
Hold, don't replyA held reply gets no automatic answer. The safeguarding lead is emailed immediately, with URGENT in the subject when needed, and the photo is kept until a person has reviewed it.
Safe outgoing lettersLetters never ask for personal details, never repeat them, never promise meetings, calls or gifts, and never suggest secrets from parents. Low-level sadness (e.g. a pet died) gets a brief, kind acknowledgement and a nudge to talk to a trusted grown-up.
PeopleWe approve every letter at launch. A named Designated Safeguarding Lead follows a written policy that sets out when to call 999, contact the council's children's services, or get advice from the NSPCC (0808 800 5000).

The automatic checks help a person notice. They never replace a person's judgement. False alarms are expected and fine. Full policy: docs/safeguarding.md.

Parent preview

Parents see every letter first, if they want to

The preview shows the letter exactly as it will print.

How it works

  1. We check and approve the letter first, so parents only see letters that have passed review.
  2. The parent gets an email: "Mary Anning has written to Maya".
  3. They approve it (posted today), ask for changes (e.g. "mention her new puppy, Pip"), or do nothing, and it posts after 48 hours.

Limits that protect our costs

  • At most 2 rewrites per child per month.
  • A rewrite reuses the same letter credit, so it never creates extra letters.
  • Change requests: 500 characters maximum, personal details removed, and they can't override accuracy or safety.
  • Every rewrite is checked by us again.
  • Child replies: at most 4 uploads per child per month.

Preview is a per-child setting, on by default at sign-up and changeable any time.

Where customers come from

  • UK home-education communities: Facebook groups, co-ops, Instagram home-ed accounts.
  • Mumsnet and Netmums: Banjo Robinson, a UK pen-pal service, ran a Mumsnet product test with 88% parent satisfaction.
  • Grandparent gifting: a prepaid "12 letters from history" plan for Christmas.
  • Word of mouth: a child opening a letter that answers their own question is our best advert.

Risks we're managing

  • Accuracy: verified fact sheets, an independent fact-checker, and human review.
  • Children's safety and data: layered safeguarding, UK GDPR, data from parents only.
  • Novelty wearing off: stories that continue, pen pals that rotate, and replies that get answered.
  • Cost abuse: caps on rewrites and uploads, and every letter is paid for first.
