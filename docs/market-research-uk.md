# UK Market Research: Letters from History

*Forem Innovations · Desk research dated 11 October 2026*

> This report adds UK detail to [`market-research-letters-from-history.md`](market-research-letters-from-history.md) (mostly about the US) and [`uk-launch.md`](uk-launch.md) (UK costs and logistics). It doesn't repeat what those documents already say.
>
> **How it was researched:** every source below was found through web search. Direct page fetches were blocked in this environment, so figures come from search results and source summaries rather than from reading each page in full. Anything marked **(unverified)** comes from a secondary or self-reported source, a page with no date, or sources that disagree. Check these figures before using them in a pitch or a plan.

## Verdict

**There is real but modest UK demand, and a clear gap. Go ahead, but expect hundreds of customers in year one, not thousands.** UK parents already pay for children's letters by post. Banjo Robinson, My Pirate Pen Pal, Tiny T's fairy pen pals and Tilly's Letters all sell at about £5 to £15 a month, and children's magazines sell at £3.50 to £11 a month. We found **no UK service that sends personalised letters from historical figures and answers the child's own letter.** The nearest concept rivals are US businesses (Historical Pen Pals, Penpaly, Pen Pals from the Past), and one US-run club, Post From The Past, posts its one-way letters from the UK. The curriculum fit is strong: Mary Anning, Florence Nightingale and the Great Fire of London are standard KS1 topics, and Walter Tull is widely taught at KS2. Children's enjoyment of writing is near a record low (28.7% in 2026), which gives parents a reason to buy. On the other side, the core home-education market is small (about 126,000 children in England), paid ads at £15 to £35 per sale could absorb most of the first month's margin, and £12 a month sits at the top of the UK letter-club price range. **A realistic first year is roughly 200 to 600 paying subscribers plus 100 to 300 gift subscriptions** (our estimate, explained in section 7). That depends on the validation tests in `validation-playbook.md` passing.

## 3. Demand signals

### 3.1 Curriculum fit (England)

The KS1 history programme asks for "the lives of significant individuals in the past who have contributed to national and international achievements", and for "events beyond living memory". Its **non-statutory examples** include *Elizabeth I and Queen Victoria*, *Christopher Columbus and Neil Armstrong*, *Rosa Parks and Emily Davison*, and *Mary Seacole and/or Florence Nightingale and Edith Cavell*. The Great Fire of London is the usual example of an event beyond living memory ([HA: Teaching famous people at KS1](https://www.history.org.uk/primary/categories/1/resource/577); [RGS](https://www.rgs.org/schools/resources-for-schools/teaching-ernest-shackleton-as-a-significant-individual-in-primary-history-at-ks1/)). We read these through schools' copies of the programme; check the wording against the GOV.UK programme of study.

It asks for stronger disciplinary knowledge and more diversity, including Black British and Asian British history. No disruption is expected before then, but check the 2027 text for our figures.

### 3.2 Children's writing (National Literacy Trust)

- **2026:** 28.7% of 8-18-year-olds enjoy writing in their free time, up from **26.6% in 2025**, the lowest since 2010 (46.8% in 2010). Only 11.9% write something daily in their free time. Girls 34.9%, boys 21.1%. Sample: 125,375 children, January to March 2026 ([NLT news](https://literacytrust.org.uk/news/our-latest-research-reveals-slight-upturn-in-childrens-writing-engagement-after-15-year-low/); [2025 report](https://literacytrust.org.uk/research-services/research-reports/children-and-young-peoples-writing-in-2025/)).

- **Why it matters:** "get your child writing for fun again" is a strong, evidence-backed message for parents. The gap between girls and boys suggests trying pen pals that appeal to boys (Brunel, Tull) in ads.

## 5. Rules that affect how we sell

*This is a checklist from public sources, not legal advice. Have a solicitor review it before launch, as `marketing-launch-plan.md` already plans.*

### 5.1 UK GDPR and children's data

- **Article 8 (consent of under-13s)** applies only when an online service is offered **directly to a child**. Our customer is the parent, so we rely on the parent's account and a lawful basis such as the contract with the parent ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/children-and-the-uk-gdpr/what-are-the-rules-about-an-iss-and-consent)).
- We still **process children's data**: name, age, interests, and the content and handwriting of their letters. The ICO expects extra care with children's data. A **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)** is the sensible step. Whether it is strictly required here is (unverified), so ask the solicitor.
- A photo of a child's letter may reveal sensitive details, such as health or family problems. Our safeguarding policy (`safeguarding.md`) and the plan to delete photos after reading both help here.
- **ICO fee:** Tier 1 is **£52 a year** (£47 by direct debit) from 17 February 2025 ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/06/fee-payable-to-the-ico-to-increase-in-february-102jzb9)). This updates the "about £50" in `uk-launch.md`.
- The **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025** received Royal Assent on 19 June 2025. One source says it puts the Children's Code principles into UK GDPR Article 25 (unverified) ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/global-data-hub/2024/february---childrens-data/likely-to-be-accessed-by-children)).

### 5.2 ICO Children's Code (Age Appropriate Design Code)

- It applies to online services **"likely to be accessed by children"**, which the ICO describes as "more probable than not". It covers any service where children are a "substantive and identifiable user group", even one not aimed at them ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/global-data-hub/2024/february---childrens-data/likely-to-be-accessed-by-children)).
- **Our position (our assessment, unverified):** the website and dashboard are for parents, and children never log in. We are probably outside the Code's core scope, but children may well look at the site with a parent. **Write down a short "likely to be accessed" assessment.** Apply the Code's defaults anyway: no profiling of children, no nudges, collect as little as possible, and show privacy information parents can understand.

### 5.3 Advertising (ASA / CAP Code)

- **Section 5 (children):** "children" means **under 16**. Ads must not tell children to buy, or to ask their parents to buy (rule 5.4.2), and must not show a child asking for the product. Promotions that require a purchase must not be aimed at children (rule 5.7) ([ASA advice](https://asa.org.uk/advice-online/children-general.html); [ASA promotions](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/children-promotional-marketing.html)).
- **What this means for us:** **aim every ad at adults** (parents and grandparents). Never write lines like "Ask Mum for a letter from Brunel!", and don't send letters with "tell your parents to upgrade" messages. The letters themselves are the product, not ads, but **never put sales messages inside a child's letter.**
- **AI:** there is **no general UK rule that AI use must be disclosed** in ads. But ads must not mislead. CAP's May 2025 guidance asks whether people would be misled without a disclosure. ASA guidance from June 2026 says AI images of real people must not suggest an endorsement ([RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/summer-2025/cap-issues-guidance-on-use-of-ai-in-ads/); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/06/18/asa-warns-on-ai-generated-content-and-deepfakes-102n4o1)). Don't use AI-made "photos" of historical figures that look real. Back up any "every fact checked" claim with records.

### 5.4 Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 (14-day cancellation)

- Online sales normally carry a **14-day cancellation right**. **Regulation 28(1)(b)** removes that right for goods "made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalised" ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/part/3)).
- **Our letters are personalised**, so the exemption *may* cover each letter once it is made. But the contract is an ongoing subscription that mixes a service with goods, and we found **no UK guidance or case on personalised subscriptions** (unverified).
- **Practical advice:** don't rely on the exemption. Our "love it or it's free" refund on the first month already goes further than the law requires, and it is simpler and better for trust. Do allow cancellation before the first letter is made.

### 5.5 DMCC Act 2024: subscription contract rules (status at October 2026)

- **Start date: January 2027.** The government brought it forward from spring 2027 in an announcement on **9 or 10 August 2026** (sources differ). The Department for Business and Trade says the requirements themselves have not changed ([TLT](https://www.tlt.com/insights-and-events/insight/dmcc-act-subscription-contracts-regime-brought-forward-by-the-pm-what-do-businesses-need-to-know); [Stephenson Harwood](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/insights/subscription-reforms-brought-forward-to-january-2027-is-your-business-ready/); [Mills & Reeve](https://www.mills-reeve.com/publications/new-subscription-rules-are-you-ready-for-january-2027/)).
- **History:** it was expected in spring 2026, then delayed to autumn 2026 at the earliest (November 2025), then to spring 2027 (April 2026), then brought forward to January 2027 ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/04/subscription-contracts)).
- **Still missing:** as of August 2026, the **secondary legislation and guidance had not been published** (TLT). Check again in November.
- **What it will require:** key information before the contract starts, **reminder notices** before renewals and before free or discounted periods end, a **cooling-off period** at the start and at renewals, and an **easy way to leave** (for example, cancelling online in one step) ([Carson McDowell](https://carson-mcdowell.com/news-insights/insights/new-subscription-contracts-regime-brought-forward)).
- **What this means for us:** families who join in November or December 2026 sign up before the new rules start. Lawyers say renewals of older contracts need checking case by case ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/new-requirements-accelerated-for-uk-consumer-subscription-contracts)). Build to the new standard now: one-click cancel in the dashboard, reminder emails before discounts end, and a clear summary before payment. The `FOUNDING30` "forever" discount and gift plans that turn into paid plans will both need reminder notices. Prepaid gifts that **don't auto-renew** are simpler.

### 5.6 Online Safety Act 2023

- The Act covers "user-to-user" services, where content uploaded by one user can be seen by **other users**, and search services ([Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/our-thinking/online-safety-act-who-does-it-apply-to/); [Bristows](https://www.bristows.com/content-hub/the-safety-net/scope-of-act/)).
- **Our assessment (unverified):** a parent's photo of a letter is seen only by us, and the answer comes back on paper. No user sees another user's content, so we are probably **out of scope**. This would change if we added a public gallery of replies, a community feed, or sharing between families. Write down the reasoning and check it again before adding any sharing features.

## 7. Risks and an honest verdict

### 7.1 Is there demand in the UK?

**Yes, at a niche level.** The evidence:

- Several UK companies sell personalised children's letters by post: Banjo Robinson (120,000 letters claimed, Mumsnet test 88% satisfied), My Pirate Pen Pal, Tiny T's and Tilly's.
- About 6 million children are aged 5-12, and about 140,000 are home-educated across the UK.
- Writing enjoyment is low and widely reported, so parents have a reason to buy.
- Our figures are taught in KS1 and KS2.
- Grandparents spend £65 or more per grandchild, and subscription gifts are an established habit.

**What we don't know:** UK search volumes, how many home-educated children are of primary age, and any proof that UK parents will pay **£12** (rather than £5-£9) for replies.

### 7.2 Realistic first-year customer numbers (our estimate)

This is reasoning, not data:

- **Home education:** if 1 in 500 home-educated children aged 5-12 in the UK subscribe, that is about 150 to 250 children. This assumes that half to two-thirds of the roughly 140,000 are of primary age, which we haven't verified.
- **Other parents (paid ads and creators):** the plan's £1,500 of Meta spend at £20-£35 per sale gives about 45 to 75 subscribers. Creators and press might add 50 to 150.
- **Gifts:** the plan's 75 gifts by the end of December looks reachable. A full year with two gift peaks (Christmas, then World Book Day and Mothering Sunday) might bring **100 to 300 gifts**.
- **Cancellations:** at the plan's 8% a month, about a third of subscribers who join in month 1 are still subscribed at month 12.

**Estimate: 200 to 600 active paying subscribers by October 2027, plus 100 to 300 gift subscriptions sold.** At an average of about £10 a month, that is roughly **£2,000 to £6,000 a month** in recurring revenue by the end of year one. That is a good small business or side project, not yet a company that needs funding. The plan's target of 300 by 31 March 2027 is ambitious but possible if Christmas gifts sell well.

### 7.3 Main risks

| Risk | Why it matters in the UK | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A US rival adds UK shipping | Historical Pen Pals and Penpaly already offer AI or reply-based history letters | Move fast on the UK advantages: KS1/KS2 figures, UK heritage partners, UK safeguarding, prices in pounds, Royal Mail |
| Price resistance at £12 | Banjo charges about £3 a letter. Magazines cost £5-£11 a month | Show a real reply in every ad. Test £9.99 against £12 in the validation stage |
| Cost per sale too high | UK Meta costs rise in Q4. £15-£35 per sale against about £9.50 a month margin | Put home-ed groups, creators and gifts first. Keep the £35 cut-off |
| Doubts about AI and safeguarding | 65% of parents worry about AI replacing human contact (BSI 2026) | Say "AI helps us write; a person checks every letter". Publish the safeguarding policy |
| DMCC rules start in January 2027 | Reminder notices, cooling-off periods, easy exit. Guidance not yet published | Build one-click cancel and reminder emails now. Make prepaid gifts non-renewing |
| Children's data | Letters can reveal sensitive details | DPIA, deleting photos, `safeguarding.md` process, a written "likely to be accessed" assessment |
| Christmas post | Peak volume; 2nd class last date about 17 December | Gift certificates with a January start. Post December letters by 11 December |
| Shrinking age group | Primary numbers are falling about 2% a year | Grow share, gifts and the classroom edition later |

### 7.4 What would change our mind

**Towards "stop" or "change course":**

- In validation, fewer than 1% of landing-page visitors join the waitlist, or fewer than 10 people pay a deposit.
- Fewer than 25% of Pen Pal children write back in the first two months. The reply is the whole difference from competitors.
- Paid cost per sale stays above £40 after £300 of spend, and free channels bring fewer than 30 families by mid-December.
- Fewer than 15% of gift subscriptions turn into paid subscriptions.
- A well-funded rival launches a UK children's version first (watch Historical Pen Pals, Penpaly, and Banjo Robinson adding real replies).

**Towards "invest more":**

- A reply rate above 40%, with parents sharing reply moments without being asked.
- Monthly cancellations below 6%.
- A heritage partner (Lyme Regis Museum, SS Great Britain) or a school trust asks for a co-branded or classroom version.
- Gift sales above 150 by Christmas.
